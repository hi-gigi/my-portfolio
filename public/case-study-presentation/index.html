Problem space

Lucid's legacy group model was originally built for document distribution, then later leveraged for group-based license allotment.

Problem space

As customer needs grew, the legacy model started to show its limits:

Tangled mental model

Sharing and license membership were mixed in one construct, leaving admins confused about setting up and managing groups.

Performance at scale

On multi-level group structures, the license allotment modal was effectively unusable.

Lack of granular controls

Growing accounts wanted controls at the group level, not single settings for the whole account.

Solution

With the goal of untangling the legacy model, I led the end-to-end design of Organizational Groups — a brand new entity that replaced its admin-governed half, offering:

01

Group-based access and licensing

02

Org-group-based sharing permissions

03

Distributed administration

Capability 1

Group-based access and licensing

For roles or departments where Lucid is a must-have, admins designate groups as licensing groups. Members get a license automatically and lose it when they're removed from the group.

Contrast: request-for-access groups, where members request a license with a valid use case and an admin approves.

Capability 2

Org-group-based sharing permissions

Granular control over who can share externally. For example, sales and marketing can share with external clients, while legal, finance, and HR cannot.

Capability 3

Distributed administration

Admin responsibilities are delegated to group leads and managers, who help central IT manage users on their team, department, or group.
